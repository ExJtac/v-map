2026 coverage is incomplete and provisional. Imported totals are dated snapshots, not certified national results. Review borders identify changes worth checking; they do not establish fraud, an error, or an election outcome. Missing data is never treated as zero.

House and Senate research progress

This work is incomplete. Numerical screening, published source context and completed investigations are different stages. A statistical flag or a routine counting update does not establish wrongdoing. The published county source cases contain context only; none is recorded as a completed investigation.

Election jurisdictions contain 3,141 House and 2,471 Senate reference-map county units. Senate includes 33 regular Class II states plus Florida and Ohio special elections. The 671 map units in states without a 2026 Senate election are outside that election scope. These are legacy map references, not current Census-county completeness: Alaska and Connecticut require special geographic treatment.

Louisiana House voting on November 3 is an open primary, with a December general election where required. Its primary is not silently compared with a historical general election. Special Senate terms also need independently qualified seat and baseline identities.

Geographic and source limits

  • Counts describe imported records, not an assertion every election or county is covered.
  • Alaska reports by election district; no unsupported county allocation is made.
  • County and district boundaries change over time; 2017 map geometry is a reference.
  • Unopposed races with no recorded vote totals remain distinct from missing sources.
  • 2026 records are dated provisional snapshots; no certified outcome or race call is inferred.

Qualified House comparisons use the reviewed state scopes listed in the methodology; other scopes remain held. Alaska’s election-district returns are not allocated to counties. Historical FIPS changes, Connecticut county definitions, Hawaii jurisdiction differences and changing congressional boundaries require explicit treatment; reference-map presence alone proves neither comparability nor completeness.
