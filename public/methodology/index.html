Election data sources and review methodology

Local data snapshot: 2026-11-07. Publication is not a live election-results feed.

V-Map preserves the distinction between reported vote counts, actual ballots, population references and comparisons that the available sources can support.

2026 coverage is incomplete and provisional. Imported totals are dated snapshots, not certified national results. Review borders identify changes worth checking; they do not establish fraud, an error, or an election outcome. Missing data is never treated as zero.

Import and source reconciliation

Source manifests identify publishers, URLs, retrieval or version dates, hashes when available, and upstream terms. Explicit source priority resolves overlapping records while preserving source-selection evidence. Known defects, missing counts, incompatible boundaries, incomplete scope and unqualified ballot rounds receive holds rather than invented values. Democratic, Republican and Other labels retain original source-party information; Other votes are never assigned to a major party for a comparison. Current party-specific growth and share screens compare identified Democratic and Republican counts only; they do not yet assess independent or other-party-specific historical changes. Qualified race-total and population measures can include their votes. A missing party-specific flag is not evidence of an ordinary pattern.

Actual reported county results are required. House county totals are constructed only when every published county-district portion has been verified. District-wide votes are never distributed across counties. A source export covering every county is not proof that counting has finished.

Provisional 2026 review-border rules

Each rule below can independently trigger a review border from a county’s own historical change. Democratic and Republican rules, gains and losses are symmetric. Source qualification applies equally to every party. Changing party labels alone must preserve assessment and flag identities while reversing directional labels; the actual-data party-swap check verifies this. Results are not adjusted to force equal flag counts or a preferred conclusion. State-peer medians and modified-z scores remain context and cannot veto these own-history rules. These thresholds are application choices, not probability estimates.

  1. Party counts: absolute change of at least 40%, at least 100 votes, while the opposing party changes by at most 15% in absolute size. Require at least 500 two-party votes in each year and 100 votes for each prior party.
  2. Reported race votes: absolute change of at least 40% and 100 votes, with at least 500 candidate/write-in race votes in each year. Overvotes, undervotes, invalid/blank/spoiled/exhausted categories and reported ballot totals are excluded. Unknown or unitemized categories withhold the total.
  3. Actual ballots per resident: absolute change of at least 10 percentage points, at least 100 explicitly reported ballots and 1,000 residents each year. Current 2026 ballots use exact 2025 population; the final/certified 2022 ballot baseline uses exact 2022 population.
  4. Race votes per resident: a separate proxy with an absolute change of at least 10pp, using qualified race-vote totals and exact population reference years. This proxy is never labeled actual ballots or eligible-voter turnout.
  5. Two-party share: an absolute Democratic/Republican share swing of at least 10pp, with the party minimums above. Other votes stay unassigned.

Residents include children and other people ineligible to vote. No ACS denominator is used. A 100% precinct-reporting figure does not establish complete ballot counting or certification. A missing source never generates a border merely for being missing.

Baseline years and qualified scopes

Governor compares 2022 with 2026, including two-year-term states; it is not labeled the immediately previous election. Senate compares the same regular Class II seat in 2020 and 2026 only where unique general, non-special records and source quality are established. The 2020 presidential-election cycle differs from the 2026 midterm, so broad race-vote changes can reflect normal cycle differences. Georgia/Louisiana November-ballot rounds require separately reviewed source-pinned contracts; all unqualified rounds and special-seat scopes remain withheld. Reviewed Florida/Ohio special contracts use the same Class III seat’s 2022 regular election and the 2026 special vacancy election for the term ending January 3, 2029; no 2020 shortcut is used.

What a flag means

A flag identifies a reported change for source review. Candidate changes, reporting progress, election cycles and geographic composition may explain it. It is not a finding that votes are wrong and does not identify a cause. Signed party directions follow the county’s actual change; neutral total/ratio flags do not acquire a party color, and conflicting directional evidence remains mixed.

Source update times, retrieval times and snapshot times remain distinct. Unknown count-update times stay unknown. The full pattern-review report and the machine-readable audit retain exact evidence, thresholds, source links, input hashes and non-assessment reasons.
